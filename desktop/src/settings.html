Where songs come from

Songs can play from files saved on this machine or stream from the archive. This decides which the app tries first.

Show what is playing on Discord

"Listening to 999 Vault" on your profile, with the song and its cover, while Discord is open. Off keeps it to yourself.

Updates

When the app opens, ask the release page whether a newer version is out. It only ever asks; you choose whether to update.

Version

Look up lyrics online

On the idle screen, ask LRCLIB for the lyrics of the song that is playing. It sends the song's title and the artist name and nothing else: no account, no id, no history. Each song is asked about once and the answer is kept on this machine. Their server sees your IP address, as any website does.

Off, or when nothing is found, the app looks for a .lrc or .txt file named like the song, beside it or in the lyrics folder of the archive.

Now playing screen

While a song plays and nothing is touched for a while, the whole window shows the cover, the title and the lyrics. The expand button on the player bar opens it whenever you like, and Escape or the X puts it away.

Crossfade

Let the end of one song blend into the start of the next. It only applies when a song would go on to another one by itself: the next in a playlist, the next in the queue, or repeat all. Pressing Next, and the end of a lone Vault song, are not faded.

Song list

The app keeps a saved copy of the archive's list of songs. Checking asks the archive what it has now, from the same place songs stream from, and shows what is new before anything changes. It sends nothing about you. Your saved songs and covers are never touched, and the old list is kept as a backup.
